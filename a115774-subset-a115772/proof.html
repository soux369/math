A115774 ⊊ A115772:
Proof of an OEIS Inclusion Conjecture

Proof note — November 27, 2026
An inclusion recorded as open in the OEIS cross-references since 2006
Repository: github.com/soux369/a115774-subset-a115772
Theorem. For every natural number i,
15·i = C(23, i)  ⟹  13·i = C(21, i),
where C is carryless (GF(2)[X]) multiplication (OEIS A048720) and the left-hand sides are ordinary integer products. Hence A115774 ⊆ A115772, and the inclusion is strict: 15 ∈ A115772 ∖ A115774.

Here A115774 = { i : 15i = C(23, i) } and A115772 = { i : 13i = C(21, i) } are rows of Karttunen's array A115872 of sets defined by congruences between ordinary and carryless products. The cross-references of the two OEIS entries had carried the inclusion “A115774 ⊆ A115772?” as an open question since 2006.

1  The state machine for one equation

Fix odd a, b and process the bits of i from the LSB. After the low t bits u of i have been processed, define the carry c = ⌊a·u / 2t⌋ and the mask m = ⌊C(b, u) / 2t⌋. Initially (c, m) = (0, 0). On input bit x ∈ {0, 1}, the next bit of a·i is (c + a·x) mod 2 and the next bit of C(b, i) is (m ⊕ b·x) mod 2. Since a and b are odd, the two bits agree for either choice of x iff c ≡ m (mod 2); the state then updates to

c′ = ⌊(c + a·x)/2⌋,    m′ = ⌊(m ⊕ b·x)/2⌋.

If all bits match, then at the end (t = L, u = i) the equation a·i = C(b, i) holds iff c = m.

3  The two key observations

Parity preservation. In all 8 states where equation 1 can proceed (c1 ≡ m1 (mod 2)), equation 2 can also proceed (c2 ≡ m2 (mod 2)).
Terminal implication. In all 14 states, c1 = m1 implies c2 = m2 (on this reachable set the two equalities are in fact equivalent).

4  Induction

Let i = Σ xt 2t satisfy 15i = C(23, i). Induction on t: after t bits, both equations have matched on the low t bits and the joint state is in the table. The base case is q₀. For the step, equation 1 matching bit t forces c1 ≡ m1 (mod 2), so the joint state is one of the 8 active states; parity preservation then gives c2 ≡ m2 (mod 2), i.e. equation 2 also matches bit t; and the table is closed under both bits, so the next joint state is again in the table. At t = L, the terminal condition for equation 1 gives c1 = m1, hence c2 = m2 by the terminal implication, which is the terminal condition for 13i = C(21, i). ∎

5  Strictness

For i = 15: 13·15 = 195 = C(21, 15), while 15·15 = 225 ≠ 221 = C(23, 15). Thus 15 ∈ A115772 ∖ A115774 and the inclusion is strict: A115774 ⊊ A115772. ∎

6  Verification

The proof is formalized in Lean 4 (file A115774SubsetA115772.lean, pure Lean 4.36.0, no Mathlib) and compiles with no errors and no sorry; #print axioms reports only the standard axioms [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. Independently, the reachable state-pair set was recomputed by BFS from the definitions and agrees with the table above exactly, and the inclusion was brute-force checked for all i < 220 (459 members of A115774, no counterexample; the member list matches the OEIS prefix).
